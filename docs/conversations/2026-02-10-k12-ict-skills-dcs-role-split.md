For professional development (PD) specifically tailored to ICT Support (Infrastructure, Helpdesk, Systems Admin) rather than just "ICT Teaching," the landscape is quite specific.

Because Dubbo Christian School is a member of Christian Education National (CEN) and an Independent School, you have access to exclusive networks that are often better than generic training courses.

Here is the best professional development, resources, and groups for your context.

1. The "Must-Join" Network

MITIE (Managing ICT in Education) This is arguably the most important organization for you to join. Unlike other groups that focus on teaching with tech, MITIE focuses on running the tech.

Why: It is a community of School ICT Managers and SysAdmins. They discuss server infrastructure, BYOD management, cybersecurity, and helpdesk ticketing specifically for schools.

Resources:

The Forum: An incredibly active mailing list/forum where you can ask, "How are you handling Year 7 iPad rollouts?" or "Is anyone else having issues with the latest filtering update?" and get answers from other NSW schools immediately.

Term Conferences: They run termly conferences (often in Sydney or hybrid) which are fantastic for networking with other private school IT staff.

2. Sector-Specific Resources (Dubbo Christian School Context)

Since your school is part of the CEN network and likely the AISNSW, you should leverage these memberships.

Christian Education National (CEN):

The ICT Network: CEN often runs specific email lists or hubs for Business Managers and ICT staff. If you aren't on this, ask your Business Manager to contact the CEN NSW state office to get you added to the relevant distribution list.

Conferences: Look out for the CEN Technical & Maintenance Conference (if running that year) or streams within their larger gatherings.

AISNSW (Association of Independent Schools NSW):

ICT Management and Leadership Conference: This is a major annual event usually held in Canberra or the Gold Coast. It is high-level PD focused on strategic planning, cyber governance, and future-proofing schools.

Online Courses: Check the AISNSW learning portal for specific modules on Child Safety/Cyber Safety which are mandatory knowledge for ICT support staff in schools.

3. Best Online Communities (For Daily Troubleshooting)

When you are stuck on a technical problem, these are the go-to places for school techs:

EduGeek.net:

What it is: The world's largest forum for educational IT professionals.

Why use it: It has a dedicated "Australia" sub-forum. If you are struggling with a specific piece of software (like PaperCut, Sentral, or TASS), someone here has already solved it.

Whirlpool Forums (Education & Whirlpool Enthusiasts):

What it is: Australia's largest tech forum.

Why use it: Great for advice on Australian ISPs, hardware procurement, and salary benchmarking for school IT roles.

4. Technical & Vendor-Specific PD

For "hard skills" (improving your technical ability), avoid generic courses and focus on the vendors your school actually uses. Common stacks in NSW Private Schools include:

Fortinet / Aruba / Meraki: If you manage the Wi-Fi/Firewall, their official "NSE" (Network Security Expert) or equivalent certifications are highly valuable.

Google for Education: Look at the Google Certified Educator Level 1 & 2 (even for support staff, it helps to know what the teachers are trying to do) and the Google Workspace Administrator training.

Microsoft Learn: Focus on Intune for Education and 365 Administration. This is critical as most schools are moving heavily into Intune for device management.

Detailed details on the MITIE travel scholarship application process are often kept within their member forum rather than on a public webpage. However, based on how MITIE operates as a community-run non-profit, the process is generally direct and personal rather than a bureaucratic automated system.

Here is how you can apply for travel assistance to attend a Term Conference from Dubbo.

1. The Application Method

Unlike large corporate grants, MITIE travel scholarships are typically handled via direct expression of interest to the Steering Committee. They do not usually have a complex web portal; they want to hear your story and your need.

The process is usually:

- Identify the event: Pick the specific Term Conference you want to attend.
- Write a formal request: Email the MITIE executive team.
- Wait for approval: The committee reviews these and approves funding for flights/accommodation.

2. Who to Contact

You should direct your application to the MITIE Secretary or the President.

Primary Contact: Michael Smith (Secretary)

Email: secretary@mitie.edu.au or contactus@mitie.edu.au

Copy in (CC): It is often good practice to CC the President, Brenton Harty (president@mitie.edu.au).

3. What to Include in Your Application

Since you are asking for school money (technically member funds), you need to make a "business case."

Draft Email Template:

Subject: Travel Scholarship Application: [Your Name] - Dubbo Christian School

Dear Michael and the MITIE Steering Committee,

I am writing to express my interest in attending the upcoming MITIE NSW Term [X] Conference on [Date].

I am currently working in ICT Support at Dubbo Christian School. As a regional member, the travel costs (flights and accommodation) are a significant barrier to attending these valuable face-to-face networking events.

Thank you for considering my request.

Best regards,

[Your Name] [Your Job Title]

4. Important Tips for Success

Be an Active Member First: If you haven't already, sign up for the free MITIE forum on their website (mitie.edu.au). If they see you are an active participant in the online discussions, they are much more likely to fund your travel.

Apply Early: These scholarships are limited. Do not wait until 2 weeks before the conference. Try to apply as soon as the conference date is announced (usually 6-8 weeks prior).

Offer Value: In your email, mention that you are happy to write a short paragraph about your experience for the newsletter, or share a "regional perspective" during the networking drinks. It shows you are willing to give back.

- VLANs & Segmentation
- VoIP/Unified Comms

Schools are device heavy. You likely have iPads for K-4, laptops for 5-12, and staff devices.

- Apple Ecosystem (Jamf / Mosyle), Apple School Manager, VPP
- Preschool kiosk mode / simplified interfaces
- Windows Environment (Intune / SCCM), Autopilot
- Google Admin Console

3. Data, Systems & Integration (The "Glue")

- SIS Management: TASS, Edumate, Sentral
- SQL/database basics and reporting
- Identity Management (SSO)
- LMS Administration
- Timetable Integration

Yeah — your list already covers a *lot*. The bits that still commonly get missed for K–12 school ICT (especially multi-campus) tend to be **service operations**, **observability**, **physical plant**, and **assessment/exam realities**.

Here are the main “missing skill buckets” I’d add:

### 1) Service management (how you run the shop)
- ITIL-ish basics: incident vs request vs problem, SLAs, priority rules, major incident comms, knowledge base hygiene, continual improvement.

### 2) Monitoring & observability (seeing trouble before it explodes)
- Network/app monitoring, alert tuning, logging, uptime reporting, capacity trends, and “is it us or the ISP?” evidence.

### 3) Automation & scripting (scaling without burnout)
- PowerShell/Python, API use, bulk changes, scheduled jobs, config-as-code mindset, repeatable builds.

### 4) Identity & access depth (beyond “create accounts”)
- RBAC/group design, least privilege, conditional access, SSO troubleshooting, passwordless, break-glass accounts, privileged access workflows.

### 5) Email/DNS/web hygiene (quietly critical)
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC, mail flow rules, phishing protection, domain/DNS management, certificate lifecycle, web categorisation edge cases.

### 6) Storage, virtualisation & performance
- Hyper-V/VMware, SAN/NAS fundamentals, backup storage design, IOPS/performance bottlenecks, lifecycle refresh planning.

### 7) Physical infrastructure (schools are brutally physical)
- Structured cabling standards, comms racks, UPS sizing/testing, power circuits, heat management, switch room discipline, “who has keys”.

### 8) Physical security systems (often lands on ICT)
- Door access control, intercoms, alarms integration, visitor management kiosks, bell/PA integration, clock systems.

### 9) Assessment/exam operations (high-stakes “it must work” days)
- Exam mode configs, locked-down browser environments, bandwidth planning, contingency plans, device spares, audit trails.

### 10) Data integration & interoperability
- Getting systems to talk: SIS ↔ LMS ↔ IDP ↔ library ↔ finance; imports/exports, APIs, rostering standards (where used), data quality checks.

### 11) Data protection controls (beyond “security”)
- DLP, sensitivity labels, retention labels, eDiscovery holds, encryption at rest/in transit, secure sharing defaults, audit log review.

### 12) Asset lifecycle & sustainability
- Procurement specs that match learning reality, warranty strategy, refresh cycles, spares pool, e-waste process, loaner agreements, loss/damage handling.

If you want a quick gut-check: the “complete” school ICT skill set isn’t just **tech** — it’s **Tech + Process + People + Place** (place = buildings, power, and campuses).
